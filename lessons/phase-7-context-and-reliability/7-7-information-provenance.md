# 7.7 Information provenance

Phase 7: Context and reliability · about 9 min · CCAR-F 5.6

When an agent combines many sources, every claim should stay linked to where it came from, through every step.

- Subagents return claims with their **source** (URL or document), a short excerpt, and the **publication date**.
- Summaries that drop those links break attribution. The final report ends up with confident, unsourced claims.
- When sources **conflict** (two credible reports give different market sizes), don't silently pick one. Show both, with sources and dates. They may differ because of timing or method.
- Separate **established** facts from **contested** ones.
- Present by content type: tables for figures and comparisons, prose for narrative.

## Key points

- Keep claim to source mappings through every step.
- Include publication dates; differences may be about timing.
- Show conflicting figures side by side with sources.
- Mark what's contested.

## Example: Conflicting figures, reported honestly

```
Canadian food delivery market, 2025:
  - CAD 9.8B (Source A, published 2026-03, survey of operators)
  - CAD 12.1B (Source B, published 2025-11, includes grocery)
The gap is mostly scope: Source B counts grocery delivery.
```

## Exam traps

- Averaging conflicting numbers or silently choosing one.
- Summarising findings into prose that loses the sources.

## Quick check

1. Two credible sources disagree on a figure. The report should...
   A. Average them
   B. Show both with sources and dates
   C. Pick the bigger one
   D. Leave it out

2. The final report has unsourced claims, though subagents had sources. Cause?
   A. Bad search
   B. Summarisation dropped the claim to source mapping
   C. Too many sources
   D. Wrong model

3. Why include publication dates?
   A. Formatting
   B. Differences between sources may come from timing
   C. Required by law
   D. To look thorough

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **B.** Readers need to see the disagreement and why.
2. **B.** Attribution has to survive each handoff.
3. **B.** A figure from 2023 and one from 2026 can both be right.

</details>
